Framework II

The Hierarchy
of Truth

Not all evidence carries equal weight. We grade every finding according to a four-tier system that separates verified fact from informed speculation — and we label everything accordingly.

The Hierarchy of Truth ensures that when we present a finding — whether it is the identification of a golden era, the documentation of a revenue leak, or the reconstruction of a competitive gap — you can trust its foundation.

Every claim carries its confidence label. Every uncertainty is disclosed. Every gap is acknowledged, not papered over.

The 3-Source Rule

No significant claim enters your report without a minimum of two independent sources — ideally three or more converging, or a single Tier 1 source standing alone. This governs every conclusion we present to your leadership team.

The Four-Tier Grading System
I
Verified Primary
The Bedrock
Contemporaneous documents with metadata. Financial records, invoices, actual spend. Third-party verification from industry publications, awards, news coverage. Multiple independent witnesses with matching stories. This is the foundation upon which all analysis rests.
II
The Probable
Highly Likely
Single witness with specific details — dates, names, amounts. Physical artifacts with provenance. Dated materials and design files. Partial documents corroborated by other sources. Treated as highly likely, requiring additional confirmation before executive briefings.
III
The Plausible
Pursue Corroboration
Single witness with vague recollections. Memory without documentation. Self-interested testimony promoting one's own role. These findings are noted, flagged, and pursued for corroboration. They may illuminate direction but cannot stand alone.
IV
The Uncertain
Cautionary Marker Only
Hearsay. Suspected falsehoods contradicted by multiple Tier 1 sources. Agenda-driven claims with no corroboration. Documented for completeness but explicitly excluded from strategic recommendations. They serve as cautionary markers, not evidence.
Framework III

The Triangulation Protocol

When sources contradict — and they will — we do not choose sides. We reconstruct truth through systematic cross-examination. Contradiction is data, not failure.

A Real Conflict · How We Resolve It
Source A Says
"We never ran television ads — too expensive for our budget in those years."
Source B Contradicts
"We ran television constantly, especially during football season — it was our biggest channel."
Archive Shows
No TV mentioned on archived website 1998–2002. Wayback Machine records confirm.
Proof Found
Three television commercials uploaded by a random user on YouTube — dated 1999, 2001, and 2003. The ads existed.
The Resolution

"The Triangulation Protocol is not about being right. It is about being accurate. Accuracy survives scrutiny. Righteousness does not."

The Resolution Framework
1
Principle One

No single source is truth. Every source has bias — memory, perspective, agenda. Truth emerges from pattern across multiple sources.

2
Principle Two

Contradiction is data, not failure. We map each source's timeframe — conflicting accounts may both be partially true, separated by time or perspective.

3
Principle Three

We document the conflict, not hide it. Ghost pages, ghost employee tracking, and the Competitive Gap analysis capture what was abandoned and when.

The Continuous Verification Model

New information emerges after delivery. A previous owner disputes a finding. An employee comes forward with new testimony.

Our Continuous Verification Model governs every post-delivery development. We review, cross-reference, and issue addenda. We never defend errors, attack sources, or ignore legitimate corrections. Accuracy is the only client.

Framework V

The 8-Dimension
Voice Profile

The Linguistic Blueprint: a forensic model of how your institution communicates. Because authenticity cannot be faked; it must be modeled.

Every institution has a voice — a pattern of vocabulary, rhythm, perspective, and proof that distinguishes it from every competitor. Most organizations have lost awareness of their own voice. We recover it, document it, and encode it for strategic deployment.

01
Vocabulary Register
Technical versus accessible. Industry jargon density. Slang and colloquialism usage. Formality level on a calibrated scale. The specific words your institution reaches for when it speaks.
02
Sentence Architecture
Average sentence length. Complexity distribution — simple, compound, complex. Rhythm patterns and fragment usage frequency. The structural DNA of your communication.
03
Humor & Tone
Humor type — witty, sarcastic, self-deprecating, observational, or absent. Enthusiasm calibration. Emotional openness. Conflict handling style. The personality that bleeds through every message.
04
Perspective & Stance
Expert versus peer positioning. Teacher versus learner stance. Optimist, realist, or critic default. Insider versus outsider language. The relationship your institution assumes with its audience.
05
Transitions & Flow
Logical connector preferences. Storytelling transition patterns. Question usage — rhetorical, direct, invitational. Pacing signals. The connective tissue that holds your narrative together.
06
CTAs & Directives
Urgency level. Command strength. Benefit framing style. Risk addressing approach. How your institution asks for action — and whether anyone listens when it does.
07
Evidence & Proof
Data usage frequency. Anecdote style — personal, observed, hypothetical. Social proof integration. Authority signaling. How your institution builds credibility without sounding desperate.
08
Visual Language
Metaphor frequency and type. Sensory detail dominance — visual, auditory, kinesthetic. Concrete versus abstract preference. Scene-setting frequency. The imagery that makes your message memorable.

"Authenticity cannot be faked; it must be modeled. The 8-Dimension Voice Profile is the model. Your institution's voice is the original. Our job is to ensure the model serves the original — and never replaces it."
