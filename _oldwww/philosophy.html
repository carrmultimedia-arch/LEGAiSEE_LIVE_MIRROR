First Principle

The Lindy Effect

The Lindy Effect states that the future life expectancy of a non-perishable entity is proportional to its current age. In plain terms: the longer something has worked, the longer it is likely to keep working.

We do not ask what the market wants today. We ask what your business has already proved it can deliver better than anyone else. The tactics that built your empire did not stop working because they became ineffective. They stopped working because they were forgotten.

This is not nostalgia. It is statistical probability. The assets buried in your history have already survived market cycles, competitive pressure, and economic downturns. They are the most robust strategic tools you own.

Narrative Drift is the silent killer of established businesses.

It begins innocently. A new marketing director arrives with fresh ideas. A competitor launches a campaign you feel compelled to answer. A consultant recommends "modernizing" your message. One decision at a time, the original logic that made you dominant is replaced by reactive noise.

After five years, no one in the building can articulate why customers originally chose you. After ten, the founding strategy exists only in the memory of retired employees — if they are still alive to recall it.
