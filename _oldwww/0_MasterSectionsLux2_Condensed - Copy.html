Section VII — Verification Science

The Hierarchy of Truth

Not all evidence carries equal weight. We grade every finding according to a four-tier system that separates verified fact from informed speculation.

Raw Stone from Earth — The Uncut Mineral Specimen
CAT-RAW-007 Evidence in its natural state, before verification and refinement
Tier 1: The Verified Primary

The Verified Primary

Contemporaneous documents with metadata. Financial records, invoices, budgets, actual spend. Third-party verification from industry publications, awards, news coverage. Multiple independent witnesses with matching stories. This is the bedrock upon which all subsequent analysis rests.

Tier 2: The Probable

The Probable

Single witness with specific details — dates, names, amounts. Physical artifacts with provenance. Dated materials, design files. Partial documents corroborated by other sources. We treat these as highly likely but requiring additional confirmation before inclusion in executive briefings.

Tier 3: The Plausible

The Plausible

Single witness with vague recollections. Memory without documentation. Self-interested testimony promoting one's own role or defending past decisions. These findings are noted, flagged, and pursued for corroboration. They may illuminate direction but cannot stand alone.

Tier 4: The Uncertain

The Uncertain

Hearsay. Suspected falsehoods contradicted by multiple Tier 1 sources. Agenda-driven claims with obvious bias and no corroboration. We document these for completeness but explicitly exclude them from strategic recommendations. They serve as cautionary markers, not evidence.

The 3-Source Rule

For any significant claim in our analysis, we require a minimum of two independent sources, ideally three or more converging sources. Alternatively, a single Tier 1 source alone is sufficient. This is the 3-Source Rule, and it governs every conclusion we present to your leadership team. We don't just 'find' information; we grade it!

The Hierarchy of Truth ensures that when we present a finding — whether it is the identification of a golden era, the documentation of a revenue leak, or the reconstruction of a competitive gap — you can trust its foundation. Every claim carries its confidence label. Every uncertainty is disclosed. Every gap is acknowledged, not papered over.

  • Contradiction is data, not failure.
  • We map each source's timeframe — both could be partially true.
  • We document the conflict, not hide it.
