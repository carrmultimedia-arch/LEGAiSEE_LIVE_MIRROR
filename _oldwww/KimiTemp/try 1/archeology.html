The Hierarchy of Truth

Tiers 1 Through 4

In a high-stakes corporate environment, a million-dollar decision cannot be built on a hunch or a fuzzy memory. This is why we treat information with the same rigor as an intelligence brief. We don't just "find" information; we grade it. Every signal we exhumed is categorized on a four-tier scale to establish a measurable confidence level before it ever touches your strategy.

Tier 1

The Verified Primary

This is the gold standard—irrefutable, documented evidence. Think of an original 1994 technical publication, a signed legal filing, or a physical 35mm master of a peak-era commercial. This is data that requires no interpretation because it exists as a primary artifact.

Tier 2

The Probable

These are highly credible secondary sources. This might be a detailed internal report from a former marketing director or a consistent pattern of press coverage from reputable trade journals during your "Golden Era." It is solid, reliable, and carries significant weight in our triangulation.

Tier 3

The Plausible

This represents reasonable assumptions supported by context. When we see your revenue spike alongside a specific cultural shift, and three different former employees vaguely recall a "handshake agreement" that changed your positioning, we have a plausible signal. It is a thread worth pulling, but it is not yet a foundation.

Tier 4

The Uncertain

This is the realm of the speculative or the conflicting. It might be a founder's memory that contradicts every financial ledger we've exhumed, or a "ghost page" on the deep web that suggests a direction you never actually took. We record these contradictions transparently rather than hiding them, because sometimes the friction between what was said and what happened is where the most valuable truth is buried.
